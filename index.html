2026-06-29 · U.S. Supreme Court

SCOTUS rules 6-3 that for-cause removal protections for SEC and CFTC commissioners are unconstitutional — Trump v. Slaughter (No. 25-332)

On June 29, 2026, the Supreme Court decided Trump v. Slaughter (No. 25-332), holding 6-3 that the Federal Trade Commission's for-cause removal provision (15 U.S.C. §41) is 'contrary to the separation of powers enshrined in the Constitution.' Authored by Chief Justice Roberts, the ruling overturns Humphrey's Executor v. United States (1935), a 91-year precedent that had permitted Congress to insulate independent agency commissioners from at-will presidential removal. Because the SEC and CFTC operate under structurally identical removal protections, the holding applies to them as well — meaning the President may now remove SEC and CFTC commissioners without cause, effective immediately. In a companion ruling the same day (Trump v. Cook, No. 25A312), the Court denied the government's application to stay the preliminary injunction protecting Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook, confirming Federal Reserve governors retain a different, more protective legal status. The opinion was revised and reposted July 1, 2026.

The Digital Asset Market Clarity Act has been placed on the U.S. Senate Legislative Calendar as Calendar No. 423, eligible for a floor vote at any time Senate leadership chooses to schedule one, following a 15-9 Senate Banking Committee markup on May 14, 2026. As reported by The Defiant on June 19, 2026, House Agriculture digital-assets subcommittee chair Rep. Cayuga Nation sues Caesars Sportsbook over mobile bets placed on tribal land — first tribal lawsuit against licensed sportsbook; novel IGRA 'where is the bet placed' geolocation theory

On June 16, 2026, the Cayuga Nation of New York filed a federal lawsuit in the Northern District of New York against American Wagering, Inc. 2026-06-18 · U.S. Senate

U.S. Senate passes 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (H.R. 6644) with statutory Fed CBDC ban through 2030; House concurrence vote pending

On June 18, 2026, the U.S. Senate invoked cloture (84-8) and passed the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (H.R. 6644). The bill includes a provision banning the Federal Reserve from issuing or creating a central bank digital currency (CBDC) or any substantially similar asset, directly or through an intermediary, through December 31, 2030. The CBDC ban defines a CBDC as a dollar-denominated digital asset that is U.S. currency, a direct liability of the Federal Reserve System, and widely available to the public; it carves out dollar-denominated, open, permissionless, and private digital currencies with privacy protections comparable to physical U.S. currency.

2026-06-18 · U.S. District Court, W.D. Michigan (Judge Paul Maloney)

W.D. Michigan denies Robinhood preliminary injunction — sports event contracts are NOT swaps; CEA does not clearly preempt state gambling law

On June 18, 2026, U.S. District Judge Paul Maloney of the Western District of Michigan denied Robinhood's motion for a preliminary injunction against Michigan state gambling enforcement in a second, near-identical ruling to the Polymarket denial issued the same day. The court held on two independent grounds: (1) sports event contracts of the type Robinhood offers are not 'swaps' under the Commodity Exchange Act, placing them outside the CFTC's exclusive swap jurisdiction; and (2) even if the contracts were swaps, it is not clearly established that the CEA would preempt Michigan's state gambling law in this context. Together with the same-day Polymarket ruling, these are the first federal court holdings that sports event contracts fall outside the CEA swap definition.
