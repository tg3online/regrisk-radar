2026-06-18 · CME Group — U.S. District Court

CME Group sues CFTC over approval of perpetual bitcoin futures (BTCPERP) on Kalshi — argues perps are swaps not futures; challenges 24-hour self-certification as APA violation

CME Group filed suit against the CFTC challenging the agency's June 2026 approval of 24/7 perpetual bitcoin futures contracts (BTCPERP) on KalshiEx via the CFTC's Regulation 40.2 self-certification process. CME's complaint presents two principal arguments: (1) that perpetual futures contracts — which have no fixed expiration date and settle continuously — are 'swaps' under the Commodity Exchange Act rather than futures, and that the CFTC therefore lacked authority to approve them for trading as futures on a designated contract market; and (2) that using the 24-hour self-certification process rather than the agency's full rule-review process for a novel product of this type violated the Administrative Procedure Act's notice-and-comment requirements. Coinbase and Kalshi had announced a planned launch of BTCPERP; that launch faces injunction risk while the litigation proceeds.

2026-06-18 · CFTC and SEC

CFTC and SEC issue joint Request for Information on derivatives data reporting modernization (Release 9257-26)

On June 18, 2026, the CFTC and SEC jointly issued Release 9257-26, a Request for Information on derivatives data reporting and regulatory technology modernization. The RFI covers three topic areas: (1) swap data repository (SDR) reporting obligations and the data standards applicable to reportable transactions; (2) real-time public reporting of swap transaction data; and (3) the potential for new data-field standards and data-exchange formats to improve regulatory oversight and reduce compliance costs for registrants. The RFI is a companion to the joint event-contracts RFI (Release 9258-26) issued the same day. The comment deadline has not yet been announced; it will appear in the Federal Register.

2026-06-18 · CFTC and SEC

CFTC and SEC issue joint Request for Information on event contracts and prediction markets (Release 9258-26)

On June 18, 2026, the CFTC and SEC jointly issued Release 9258-26, a Request for Information on event-based contracts and the application of existing regulatory frameworks to prediction markets. The RFI asks market participants and the public to comment on questions including: when event contracts constitute swaps subject to CFTC jurisdiction, futures subject to CFTC jurisdiction, or securities subject to SEC jurisdiction; how existing statutory definitions should apply to contracts on sports, political, and other event outcomes; and whether the current dual-agency framework requires clarification or new rulemaking. This is the first joint inter-agency RFI on the jurisdictional classification of event contracts and prediction markets. The comment deadline has not yet been announced; it will appear in the Federal Register.

2026-06-18 · CFTC

CFTC enters final consent order against Celsius Network and CEO Mashinsky — civil fraud, CEL token manipulation, and unregistered pool charges resolved; DOJ criminal case continues

On June 18, 2026, the CFTC entered a final consent order against Celsius Network LLC and its former CEO Alex Mashinsky resolving civil enforcement claims arising from the 2022 collapse of the Celsius crypto-lending platform. The consent order resolves three enforcement theories: (1) fraudulent misrepresentation of deposit safety and liquidity practices to retail customers — Celsius represented to depositors that their assets were safe and liquid when, in fact, the platform faced material undisclosed risks; (2) market manipulation through coordinated trading in Celsius's native CEL token, which the CFTC classified as a commodity; and (3) operating as an unregistered commodity pool operator (CPO) without CFTC registration, in violation of the CEA. The consent order resolves the CFTC's civil enforcement action; the U.S. Department of Justice's separate criminal case against Mashinsky continues. Celsius Network ceased operations in 2022 and remains in bankruptcy proceedings.

2026-06-18 · Federal Reserve, OCC, FDIC, NCUA, and Treasury (FinCEN)

Five federal agencies propose a customer identification program rule for payment stablecoin issuers (GENIUS Act)

On June 18, 2026, the Federal Reserve Board — jointly with the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, the National Credit Union Administration, and the Treasury Department's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) — requested comment on a proposed rule that would require certain permitted payment stablecoin issuers (PPSIs) to maintain an effective customer identification program (CIP), with requirements comparable to the customer identification program requirements for banks and credit unions, implementing the GENIUS Act. According to the proposal, the program must include reasonable procedures for verifying the identity of any person seeking to open an account to the extent reasonable and practicable; maintaining records of the information used to verify a person's identity, including name, address, and other identifying information; and determining whether the person appears on any government-provided lists of known or suspected terrorists or terrorist organizations. Comments are due 60 days after publication in the Federal Register. The agencies' 130-page proposal also asks whether any CIP requirement should be extended to secondary market activity; Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr issued a statement saying the GENIUS Act framework 'does not do enough so far to address the risks of illicit finance conducted through secondary market transactions in payment stablecoins.'

2026-06-18 · State of Illinois (Gov. JB Pritzker)

Illinois enacts first-in-nation digital-asset transaction tax (SB 3019 / Digital Asset Tax Act)

Illinois Governor JB Pritzker signed Senate Bill 3019, the Digital Asset Tax Act (DATA), as part of the state's $55.9 billion budget, making Illinois the first U.S. state to impose a transaction-based tax on digital-asset activity. The law imposes a 0.2% privilege tax on brokers who exchange, transfer, or store digital assets for customers in the state; it applies to brokers with a physical presence in Illinois or an economic nexus (more than $100,000 in gross receipts from Illinois customers), takes effect January 1, 2027, and is projected to generate roughly $60 million. The Crypto Council for Innovation has requested a line-item veto of the article establishing the levy, and The Digital Chamber and the Illinois Blockchain Association issued a joint letter opposing the measure; critics note no comparable state transaction tax applies to stocks, bonds, or derivatives.

2026-06-18 · U.S. Senate Banking Committee (Congress)

Congress folds a statutory Fed CBDC ban (through 2030) into the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (H.R. 6644)

On June 16, 2026, the leaders of the Senate Banking and House Financial Services Committees — Sen. Tim Scott, Sen. Elizabeth Warren, Rep. French Hill, and Rep. Maxine Waters — released updated bill text for the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (H.R. 6644), a combined Senate, House, and White House package. According to the release and reporting on the text, the package folds in a statutory ban on a Federal Reserve central bank digital currency (CBDC): it amends the Federal Reserve Act to provide that the Fed 'may not issue or create' a CBDC or any substantially similar asset, directly or through an intermediary, through December 31, 2030; defines a CBDC as a dollar-denominated digital asset that is U.S. currency, a direct liability of the Federal Reserve System, and widely available to the public; and carves out a dollar-denominated, open, permissionless, and private digital currency with privacy protections comparable to physical U.S. currency. The reconciled text returns to the Senate floor for final action; a prior version passed the Senate 89-10 in March and the House passed its amended version 396-13 in May.

2026-06-17 · U.S. District Court, W.D. Michigan

W.D. Michigan denies Polymarket PI — court rules sports event contracts are NOT 'swaps' under the CEA; CEA may not preempt state gambling law even if they were

On June 17, 2026, the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Michigan denied Polymarket's motion for a preliminary injunction against enforcement of Michigan state gambling law. The court rested its denial on two independent holdings: (1) sports event contracts of the type Polymarket offers are not 'swaps' under the Commodity Exchange Act, meaning the CFTC's exclusive swap jurisdiction does not cover them; and (2) even if the contracts were classified as swaps, it is not clearly established that the CEA would preempt Michigan's state gambling law in this context. The case continues on the merits. The ruling is the first federal court holding that sports event contracts fall outside the CEA swap definition and that CEA preemption of state gambling law is not clearly established — two holdings directly contrary to the CFTC's preemption position.

2026-06-17 · NYDFS

NYDFS proposes stablecoin regulation aligning New York's framework with the federal GENIUS Act

On June 9, 2026, the New York State Department of Financial Services, under Acting Superintendent Kaitlin Asrow, proposed a regulation to build on New York's existing stablecoin framework and align it with the federal GENIUS Act. The proposal carries forward DFS's prior requirements for U.S. dollar-backed stablecoins issued under its oversight — backing and redeemability, permissible reserves, and independent audits — and adds new federal provisions, including setting maximum amounts of reserves that can be held at any one custodian and requiring issuers to adopt risk-management programs covering internal controls, information security, internal audit, asset growth, earnings, insider and affiliate transactions, and service-provider arrangements. The proposed regulation is subject to a 10-day preproposal comment period beginning June 9, 2026, followed by a 60-day comment period upon publication in the State Register; the final regulation would take effect at the same time as the GENIUS Act becomes effective, with a one-year transition period for existing New York-licensed issuers. The Department's June 2022 stablecoin guidance remains in effect until then.

2026-06-16 · CFTC

CFTC issues Request for Information on fintech innovation, AI, and digital asset technologies (Release 9254-26)

On June 16, 2026, the CFTC issued Release 9254-26, a Request for Information on fintech innovation, artificial intelligence, and digital asset technologies. The RFI covers five topic areas: (1) decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols and their interaction with CFTC-regulated markets, including whether DeFi participants may be acting as unregistered intermediaries; (2) artificial intelligence and machine learning applications in trading, risk management, and regulatory compliance, including automated order routing and AI-generated trading strategies; (3) real-world asset (RWA) tokenization and its implications for CFTC-regulated derivatives markets; (4) payment stablecoins and their use as margin, collateral, or settlement assets in derivatives transactions; and (5) cybersecurity and operational resilience for digital-asset market participants. The comment window is currently open; the deadline will appear in the Federal Register. 2026-06-16 · Pennsylvania Supreme Court

Pennsylvania Supreme Court rules skill games are slot machines; 120-day enforcement stay issued

On June 15, 2026, the Pennsylvania Supreme Court issued its majority opinion in J-97-2025 (Pace-O-Matic v. Pennsylvania), ruling that skill game machines 'are subject to both the Gaming Act and the Crimes Code,' overturning a Commonwealth Court ruling the Justices called 'deeply flawed.' Justice David Wecht wrote that the POM device is 'several times over' a slot machine and the legal definition 'describes the POM device with precision.' The Court issued a 120-day enforcement stay during which law enforcement cannot take action against owners and operators, and noted the General Assembly 'remains free at any time to take whatever legislative action it may deem appropriate.' Pace-O-Matic stated it is 'disappointed,' said the impact on 10,000+ small businesses and fraternal clubs presents an 'impossible choice,' and will pursue a legislative solution; bipartisan bills SB 1079 (Sen. Yaw) and HB 2213 (Rep. Burgos) are cited. Pennsylvania AG Dave Sunday called the ruling 'a significant victory for consumers, taxpayers, and the rule of law.' An estimated 70,000+ skill game machines operate in bars, VFWs, restaurants, and fraternal clubs statewide.

2026-06-16 · Financial Technology Association v. Tennessee — Davidson County Chancery Court

Fintech trade group sues Tennessee to block remittance tax (HB2502)

As reported by The Center Square (and corroborated by Law360, CU Today, and Fintech Business Weekly), the Financial Technology Association filed suit in Davidson County (Tennessee) Chancery Court seeking an injunction to block HB2502, a law signed by Gov. Bill Lee in May 2026 that adds a $10 fee on monetary wire transfers to another country plus a 2% tax on transfers over $500 (effective Jan. 1, 2027; projected to raise more than $91 million in FY2026-27). The FTA argues the law is unconstitutional under the dormant Commerce Clause because it singles out international payments for special taxation, and its complaint states members will have to modify transaction-processing systems, compliance software, and customer-facing interfaces, incurring nonrecoverable compliance costs before year-end or risk penalties. FTA president and CEO Penny Lee said, 'when a state singles out international payments for special taxation, that is unconstitutional, plain and simple.'

2026-06-16 · Delaware General Assembly (HB 441) / New Jersey Legislature (S-2141)

Delaware (HB 441) and New Jersey (S-2141) advance statewide crypto-ATM bans

As reported by The Defiant (and corroborated by Cointelegraph, CryptoNews, and Grafa), Delaware and New Jersey have each advanced legislation to ban cryptocurrency ATMs statewide, citing fraud losses. Delaware's House Bill 441 cleared the House Economic Development Committee and passed the House; it would require existing kiosks to go dark upon enactment and be physically removed within 90 days, bar cashier-assisted workarounds, and expose operators to unlawful-trade-practice charges plus victim-refund and forfeiture obligations. New Jersey's Senate Bill S-2141 cleared the Senate Commerce Committee unanimously and heads to the full chamber. Sponsors cited FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center data of 13,400 crypto-ATM-scam complaints in 2025 and more than $388 million in losses (a 58% year-over-year increase), with more than half of complaints involving victims over 60; operator CoinFlip urged regulation with transaction monitoring instead of prohibition. Tennessee, Indiana, and Minnesota already have statewide bans.

2026-06-16 · Gary Gensler (amicus) — U.S. Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit

Gensler files Sixth Circuit amicus arguing sports prediction markets are not 'swaps'

As reported by The Defiant (and corroborated by DeFi Rate), on 2026-06-11 former CFTC and SEC chair Gary Gensler filed an amicus brief with the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit in KalshiEX LLC v. Schuler — Kalshi's appeal of an Ohio district court's denial of a preliminary injunction — arguing that sports-event prediction-market contracts fall outside the statutory definition of a 'swap' under Dodd-Frank and are therefore not within the CFTC's exclusive jurisdiction. The brief contends the swap definition centers on hedging economic risk and that reading it to cover sports bets would imply every off-exchange wager since October 2012 had been illegal. The position contradicts the CFTC's own May 12, 2026 amicus asserting exclusive jurisdiction.
