2026-07-14 · U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC)

CFTC stays KalshiEX rule change and uses emergency authority to order fulfillment of Michigan trades a state court directed be canceled (Release 9267-26)

On July 14, 2026, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission announced (Release 9267-26) that it exercised its authority to stay an emergency rule change proposed by KalshiEX, LLC in response to a Michigan state court order directing the company to cancel certain previously executed trades involving Michigan residents, and separately exercised its emergency authority to order KalshiEX to fulfill the open trades in accordance with its normal practices. Chairman Michael S. Selig stated that 'a state cannot force a DCM to violate its obligations, and federal law does not permit a DCM to discriminate against a state's residents,' and called canceling already-executed trades an 'unprecedented step' that risks a cascading effect on the marketplace. The Commission noted that Michigan is the first state to attempt to interfere directly with executed derivatives transactions and that it has filed lawsuits against Arizona, Connecticut, Illinois, Kentucky, Minnesota, New Mexico, New York, Rhode Island, and Wisconsin to protect its jurisdiction.

2026-07-14 · U.S. Department of the Treasury / HM Treasury

U.S.-UK Transatlantic Taskforce publishes recommendations on capital markets and digital assets, plus a joint statement on stablecoins

On July 14, 2026, the U.S. Department of the Treasury and HM Treasury jointly published recommendations of the Transatlantic Taskforce for Markets of the Future (Treasury press release SB0560). Treasury states the recommendations aim to deepen cross-border financial activity between the United States and the United Kingdom, reduce unnecessary frictions, and advance open, market-based standards, and that they identify opportunities to enhance cross-border capital raising, update supervisory cooperation, and provide clarity for tokenized financial activity. 2026-07-13 · Independent Community Bankers of America / American Bankers Association

ICBA, ABA and 76 state banking associations urge Senate leaders to tighten CLARITY Act Section 404 stablecoin yield provisions

As announced by the Independent Community Bankers of America on July 13, 2026, the American Bankers Association and ICBA, joined by 76 state associations, sent a letter to Senate Majority Leader John Thune and Minority Leader Charles Schumer urging targeted changes to the CLARITY Act to provide greater certainty that payment stablecoins cannot function as substitutes for bank deposits. The associations wrote that 'significant questions remain regarding whether the current language in Section 404 provides sufficient clarity and certainty to achieve that objective' and that 'ambiguities within the bill could encourage stablecoin arrangements to effectively function as substitutes for deposits, despite Congress's longstanding and clearly stated intent that payment stablecoins should serve as transaction tools rather than store-of-value products.' They urge revisions clarifying the prohibition on interest and yield, replacing the bill's 'functional and economic equivalent' standard with a 'substantially similar' standard, and removing language they believe could create ambiguity regarding rewards tied to stablecoin balances, duration or tenure. The underlying letter is linked from the announcement and was not independently retrieved.

2026-07-07 · U.S. District Court, Southern District of New York

SDNY judge denies Kalshi's preliminary injunction against the New York State Gaming Commission; holds the CEA does not preempt state gambling laws as applied to sports-event contracts

On July 7, 2026, U.S. District Judge Analisa Torres (S.D.N.Y.) denied KalshiEX LLC's motion for a preliminary injunction in KalshiEX LLC v. Williams (No. 1:25-cv-08846), a suit against the New York State Gaming Commission. As reported by The Block (July 8, 2026), Kalshi has appealed the decision to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit.

2026-07-03 · U.S. Supreme Court

SCOTUS rules 6-3 that for-cause removal protections for SEC and CFTC commissioners are unconstitutional (Trump v. Slaughter, No. 25-332)

On June 29, 2026, the Supreme Court of the United States issued its 6-3 opinion in Trump v. Slaughter (No. 25-332), authored by Chief Justice Roberts, overturning Humphrey's Executor v. United States (1935). The Court held that the for-cause removal protection in the FTC's enabling statute — and by structural extension the SEC and CFTC, which operate under identical removal provisions — is contrary to the separation of powers under the Constitution. Effective immediately, the President may remove SEC and CFTC commissioners without cause. A companion order in Trump v. Cook (No. 25A312, June 29, 2026) denied a government application to stay a preliminary injunction protecting Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook; the Federal Reserve's statutory structure was not directly addressed by the majority opinion. The opinion is published on the Court's official slip opinion list for October Term 2025.
