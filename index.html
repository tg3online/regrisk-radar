2026-06-23 · U.S. Department of the Treasury / Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC)

OFAC sanctions nine individuals and 26 entities linked to Prince Group Transnational Criminal Organization — virtual currency scam compounds; coordinated with FinCEN H-Pay Service rule amendment

On June 23, 2026, the U.S. Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) sanctioned nine individuals and 26 entities linked to the Prince Group Transnational Criminal Organization (Prince Group TCO), including TCO leadership, investors in scam compounds, and front companies. The Prince Group TCO operates virtual currency investment scam compounds in Southeast Asia and is involved in pig-butchering and other fraud targeting American victims. The action builds on OFAC's October 14, 2025 designation of the Prince Group as a TCO (coordinated at that time with the United Kingdom's Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office) and was taken concurrently with FinCEN's proposed amendment to its October 2025 Huione Group Final Rule to include H-Pay Service PLC and successor entities. According to the Treasury press release, Huione Group served as a critical node for laundering proceeds of cyber heists and virtual currency investment scams and was used by the Prince Group to transfer and consolidate scam-derived assets. Secretary of the Treasury Scott Bessent stated: 'Scam centers in Southeast Asia steal billions of dollars from American victims each year. The Trump Administration is united in its efforts to dismantle these overseas criminal enterprises, and Treasury will continue using its tools to disrupt the networks behind this egregious fraud and protect Americans.'

2026-06-23 · Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN)

FinCEN proposes to extend Huione Group Section 311 special measures to H-Pay Service PLC and successor entities — NPRM amending October 2025 Final Rule

On June 23, 2026, the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) issued a notice of proposed rulemaking (NPRM) to amend its October 2025 Huione Group Final Rule, which imposed Section 311 special measures (severing from the U.S. financial system) on Huione Group, to also cover H-Pay Service PLC and any successor entity of the Huione Group. FinCEN identified H-Pay Service PLC as a Cambodia-based entity that serves as a successor to Huione Pay, a Huione Group subsidiary previously identified by FinCEN as a primary money laundering concern. According to the FinCEN news release, Huione Group served as a critical node for laundering proceeds of cyber heists and virtual currency investment scams and was used by the Prince Group Transnational Criminal Organization to transfer and consolidate scam-derived assets. The proposed rule would close off avenues for Huione Group to circumvent the October 2025 Final Rule by operating through successor entities. The action was coordinated with OFAC's simultaneous designation of nine individuals and 26 entities linked to the Prince Group TCO.

2026-06-22 · Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), jointly with OCC, Federal Reserve, FDIC, and NCUA

Stablecoin issuer Customer Identification Program proposed rule published in the Federal Register, opening the 60-day comment period (GENIUS Act implementation)

On June 22, 2026, the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), together with the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, and the National Credit Union Administration, published in the Federal Register a joint notice of proposed rulemaking titled 'Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuer Customer Identification Program' (document 2026-12460), implementing the GENIUS Act. According to the Federal Register notice, the proposal would require certain permitted payment stablecoin issuers to maintain a customer identification program with requirements comparable to those for banks and credit unions, including reasonable procedures for verifying the identity of any person seeking to open an account, maintaining records of the information used to verify identity, and determining whether the person appears on any government-provided lists of known or suspected terrorists or terrorist organizations. Comments are due 60 days after publication in the Federal Register (closing on or about August 21, 2026). The 130-page proposal also asks whether any CIP requirement should be extended to secondary market activity.

A closed-door bipartisan ethics meeting over the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act collapsed on June 9, 2026. The meeting included Senators Gillibrand, Gallego, Moreno, and Lummis, along with White House Crypto Council Executive Director Patrick Witt. Polymarket traders price 2026 passage at approximately 48%, down from 74% one month earlier; Galaxy Research estimates 50-50 passage odds.

2026-06-18 · U.S. House Committee on House Administration (Chairman Bryan Steil, WI-01)

Rep. Steil introduces the 'Stop Lawmakers from Predicting Act' — would bar members of Congress, spouses, and dependent children from political/policy wagers on prediction markets

On June 18, 2026, Committee on House Administration Chairman Bryan Steil (WI-01) introduced the Stop Lawmakers from Predicting Act, which would prohibit Members of Congress, their spouses, and their dependent children from using prediction markets to wager on a specific government policy, government action, or political outcome (bets on unrelated events such as sports remain permitted). Per the Committee's press release, violators must pay a fee equal to $2,000 or ten percent of the value of the prohibited transaction (whichever is greater) plus the net gain realized; Members may not use their Members' Representational Allowance, Senate personnel and office expense account, or political contributions or donations to pay the fine; and Members who resign or retire without paying may be referred to DOJ for civil enforcement. The bill builds on the Stop Insider Trading Act advanced by the Committee on January 14, 2026. Speaker Mike Johnson and President Trump back the bill. The measure echoes a Senate rule adopted earlier in 2026 barring senators and staff from prediction-market betting.

2026-06-16 · Cayuga Nation (plaintiff) — U.S. District Court, Northern District of New York

Cayuga Nation sues Caesars Sportsbook over mobile bets placed on tribal land — first tribal lawsuit against licensed sportsbook; novel IGRA 'where is the bet placed' geolocation theory

On June 16, 2026, the Cayuga Nation of New York filed a federal lawsuit in the Northern District of New York against American Wagering, Inc. (d/b/a Caesars Sportsbook), alleging that Caesars accepted mobile sports wagers from people physically located on the Cayuga reservation between January 8, 2022 and July 15, 2025, without tribal authorization, a gaming compact, or federal oversight. The complaint alleges violations of the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act (IGRA): it argues that a mobile sports wager is placed at the bettor's physical location, not at the operator's server, and therefore betting accepted from within the reservation constitutes Class III gaming on Indian lands, requiring a tribal gaming ordinance and a federally approved tribal-state compact that the Cayuga Nation does not have for sports betting. The Nation sent Caesars a cease-and-desist letter on June 20, 2025; Caesars subsequently agreed to geofence the reservation but declined to provide a full accounting of prior revenue. The complaint seeks damages, an accounting of reservation-bet revenue, and a declaration that Caesars' mobile operations on Cayuga lands were unauthorized. Gaming attorney Daniel Wallach identified this as the first known case of a tribe suing a state-licensed sportsbook over online bets placed from Indian lands. The Cayuga Nation operates Class II gaming venues (LakeSide Entertainment) in New York's Finger Lakes region.

On June 18, 2026, the U.S. Senate invoked cloture (84-8) and passed the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (H.R. 6644). The bill includes a provision banning the Federal Reserve from issuing or creating a central bank digital currency (CBDC) or any substantially similar asset, directly or through an intermediary, through December 31, 2030. The CBDC ban defines a CBDC as a dollar-denominated digital asset that is U.S. currency, a direct liability of the Federal Reserve System, and widely available to the public; it carves out dollar-denominated, open, permissionless, and private digital currencies with privacy protections comparable to physical U.S. currency. Senate passage follows the release of reconciled bill text on June 16, 2026 by Sen. Tim Scott, Sen. Elizabeth Warren, Rep. French Hill, and Rep. Maxine Waters, and a Motion to Proceed vote of 87-8 earlier in the day. The amended bill returns to the House for concurrence in the Senate amendment (H.Res.1299 provides the rule for consideration of the concurrence). Prior votes: Senate 89-10 in March (prior version); House 396-13 in May. 2026-06-18 · Federal Reserve, OCC, FDIC, NCUA, and Treasury (FinCEN)

Five federal agencies propose a customer identification program rule for payment stablecoin issuers (GENIUS Act)

On June 18, 2026, the Federal Reserve Board — jointly with the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, the National Credit Union Administration, and the Treasury Department's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) — requested comment on a proposed rule that would require certain permitted payment stablecoin issuers (PPSIs) to maintain an effective customer identification program (CIP), with requirements comparable to the customer identification program requirements for banks and credit unions, implementing the GENIUS Act. According to the proposal, the program must include reasonable procedures for verifying the identity of any person seeking to open an account to the extent reasonable and practicable; maintaining records of the information used to verify a person's identity, including name, address, and other identifying information; and determining whether the person appears on any government-provided lists of known or suspected terrorists or terrorist organizations. Comments are due 60 days after publication in the Federal Register. The agencies' 130-page proposal also asks whether any CIP requirement should be extended to secondary market activity; Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr issued a statement saying the GENIUS Act framework 'does not do enough so far to address the risks of illicit finance conducted through secondary market transactions in payment stablecoins.'

Illinois enacts first-in-nation digital-asset transaction tax (SB 3019 / Digital Asset Tax Act)

Illinois Governor JB Pritzker signed Senate Bill 3019, the Digital Asset Tax Act (DATA), as part of the state's $55.9 billion budget, making Illinois the first U.S. state to impose a transaction-based tax on digital-asset activity. The law imposes a 0.2% privilege tax on brokers who exchange, transfer, or store digital assets for customers in the state; it applies to brokers with a physical presence in Illinois or an economic nexus (more than $100,000 in gross receipts from Illinois customers), takes effect January 1, 2027, and is projected to generate roughly $60 million. On June 9, 2026, the New York State Department of Financial Services, under Acting Superintendent Kaitlin Asrow, proposed a regulation to build on New York's existing stablecoin framework and align it with the federal GENIUS Act.
