2026-08-07 · Commodity Futures Trading Commission

CFTC staff letter tells event-contract markets that bookmaker-style 'American odds' pricing displays are likely to mislead, with receipt to be confirmed by August 31, 2026

In Release 9278-26, dated August 7, 2026, the CFTC stated that its Division of Market Oversight and Market Participants Division "recently issued a letter to remind Commission-regulated entities involved in the listing, soliciting, or acceptance of event contracts of their responsibility not to mislead consumers, including the obligation to display clear and accurate pricing information for derivatives products." The release stated that staff "warned that displaying pricing information in the 'American' odds format used by casino gambling bookmakers is likely to mislead market participants about the nature of the transaction and may deprive users of access to indicia of market depth and pricing impact," and that "displaying misleading pricing information in connection with any regulated product risks violating federal law prohibiting the use of manipulative devices." The underlying letter, addressed to "CFTC-Regulated Entities and Affiliates" under the subject "Misleading or Deceptive Practices" and signed by DJ Hennes, Director of the Market Participants Division and Acting Director of the Division of Market Oversight, cites the prohibition on misleading practices in 7 U.S.C. 9 and Commission Regulation 180.1, notes that the same requirements are "mirrored in other rules governing CFTC-regulated entities and their affiliates, for example in National Futures Association Compliance Rule 2-29," and states that designated contract markets "have an obligation to protect market participants from abusive, noncompetitive, or unfair actions by other parties involved in the transaction" under Core Principle 12. The letter states that the divisions "request that CFTC-regulated entities that list, solicit, or accept orders for event contract derivatives products review their pricing displays, marketing material, and other information, as well as information displayed or used by their partners and affiliates and confirm receipt of this letter by August 31, 2026," directing introducing brokers and futures commission merchants to confirm to MPDAlerts@CFTC.gov and designated contract markets to confirm to DMOLetters@CFTC.gov.

2026-08-07 · Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), U.S. Department of the Treasury

FinCEN's renewed Minnesota Geographic Targeting Order is effective August 11, 2026 and ends February 6, 2027, and requires money transmitters to report whether a transmittal was a convertible virtual currency transmission

The order document "Geographic Targeting Order Imposing Recordkeeping and Reporting Requirements on Certain Financial Institutions in Minnesota" (31 CFR Part 1010), signed by FinCEN Deputy Director Jimmy L. Kirby, states: "DATES: Effective Date: This action is effective on August 11, 2026," and "The terms of this Order are effective on August 11, 2026, and end on February 6, 2027." A "Covered Business" is "any bank, as defined in 31 CFR 1010.100(d), or a money transmitter, as defined in 31 CFR 1010.100(ff)(5), with a branch, subsidiary, or office located in the Covered Geographic Area," which the order defines as "Hennepin County and Ramsey County, Minnesota." A Covered Transaction is a funds transfer of $3,000 or more where the originator or transmittor provides an address in the covered area, is not publicly traded on an SEC-regulated exchange, is not a financial institution subject to Bank Secrecy Act anti-money-laundering program requirements, and where "[e]ither the beneficiary or recipient is located outside of the United States or the financial institution used by the beneficiary or recipient to receive the funds is located outside of the United States." Money transmitters must report the recipient's name, address, date of birth, phone number and email address; whether the transmittal was sourced from currency, check, credit or debit card, or other; whether the source of funds includes payments from any federal, state or local government contract or benefit program; "The form of transmittal (wire, convertible virtual currency transmission, ledger entry, or other)"; and "If the transmittal is a ledger entry that tracks credits and debits with hawaladars located internationally, whether the money transmitter uses cash couriers to settle those debits." Reports are due "by the end of the month following the month in which the Covered Transaction took place," filed as a CSV through FinCEN's Financial Industry Portal under file type "Special Measures" with identifier FIN-65547-X3M6T. Records must be retained "for a period of five years from the last day that this Order is effective." The order states that a Covered Business and "any of its officers, directors, employees, and agents, may be liable, without limitation, for civil or criminal penalties for willfully violating any of the terms of this Order," and that it "is being publicly issued, and its terms are not confidential." The information collection carries OMB control number 1506-0056.

2026-08-07 · Office of the New York State Attorney General

NY Attorney General issues consumer alert on "gold bar scams" that move victims' savings out of bank accounts

On August 7, 2026 New York Attorney General Letitia James issued a consumer alert warning of "increasingly common 'gold bar scams'" targeting seniors. The alert states the scams "usually start with a fake pop-up message on victims' computers, claiming their computer or financial accounts have been compromised or linked to criminal activity," that scammers "convince the victim to give them remote access to their computer and then persuade the victim to convert their money into gold bars to protect their life savings," and that "These gold bars are later collected and laundered by the scammers." According to the alert, "The New York City Police Department (NYPD) has investigated more than 100 gold bar scam cases over the past two years, with total losses exceeding $100 million." The alert states that victims are connected to "an individual posing as a law enforcement officer or government investigator" who tells them they "must withdraw their savings and purchase gold bars or coins from legitimate dealers and turn these bars over to the purported authorities," that "Victims are instructed to keep the matter confidential and not discuss it with bank employees or family members," and that "The scammers will then use couriers to collect the gold from the victim." Published consumer tips include "Never move money out of your bank account at the urging of someone over the phone" and, if someone claims there is an issue with a bank account, to "hang up and contact your financial institution by calling the number printed on your bank statement instead." The alert directs New York City residents to contact their local NYPD precinct and others to file a complaint with the OAG or call 1-800-771-7755.

2026-08-07 · Federal Trade Commission

FTC issues a policy statement that it will not pursue disparate-impact or 'unfair discrimination' claims

On August 7, 2026 the Federal Trade Commission announced "a policy statement clarifying that the Commission will not pursue claims based on disparate-impact or 'unfair discrimination' theories." Chairman Andrew N. Ferguson is quoted in the release: "Disparate-impact claims are nearly impossible to square with our colorblind Constitution. They impose liability for discrimination without any evidence that anyone intended to discriminate, which pushes businesses to make race-based decisions in order to avoid liability. The Commission never had authority to impose disparate-impact liability. Today, we announce that the Commission will never do so again." The FTC states that the policy statement follows a Presidential executive order on "Restoring Equality of Opportunity and Meritocracy," and that the statement explains the Commission lacks the statutory authority to consider claims under this theory. On what remains, the release states: "The statement sets forth how the Commission will pursue discrimination claims in appropriate contexts. For example, it will continue to assert disparate-treatment claims under the Equal Credit and Opportunity Act, but it will treat Section 5 of the FTC Act as the consumer-protection statute it has always been." The FTC also states that under the new policy it reviewed past decisions based on statistical analyses designed to show disparate-impact liability and entered into agreements to modify certain compliance-related obligations for Napleton Inc., Passport Auto Group and an individual previously associated with Coulter Motor Company LLC. The release states the Commission vote authorizing the policy statement and approving the agreements was 2-0.

2026-08-07 · Federal Trade Commission

FTC policy statement text: the Commission will no longer pursue disparate-impact claims in any context and will bring no antidiscrimination claims under Section 5, while the statement disclaims binding effect

The FTC "Policy Statement Regarding Disparate-Impact Claims and 'Unfair Discrimination' Claims," listed in the FTC Legal Library under date August 7, 2026, states in its Introduction: "The FTC has previously pursued disparate-impact claims in two contexts: under Section 5 of the FTC Act (where they have been styled as 'unfair discrimination' claims), and under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act ('ECOA'). In both instances, the FTC acted ultra vires. Section 5 contains no antidiscrimination cause of action, let alone a disparate-impact one. And while ECOA does prohibit intentional discrimination, it does not authorize disparate-impact claims." It concludes: "Accordingly, in light of (1) the FTC's lack of statutory authority to bring disparate-impact claims and (2) the fundamental legal and policy defects of disparate-impact liability laid out in President Trump's executive order, the FTC will no longer pursue disparate-impact claims in any context. In addition, because Section 5 does not create an antidiscrimination cause of action, the Commission will not bring any such claims under Section 5." The statement identifies the order as "Exec. Order No. 14281, Restoring Equality of Opportunity and Meritocracy, 90 Fed. Reg. 17537 (Apr. 23, 2025)" and says it directs agencies, including the FTC, to "deprioritize enforcement of all statutes and regulations to the extent they include disparate-impact liability." On what survives, the statement says "Intentional discrimination can be combatted through traditional disparate-treatment claims, and the Commission remains firmly committed to bringing such claims where the law allows," citing a July 17, 2025 concurring statement in the Asbury Automotive Group matter (Matter No. 2223135) stating the Commission "will not hesitate to bring disparate-treatment claims under ECOA where the facts support such claims." Footnote 1 states: "This Policy Statement does not confer any rights on any person and does not operate to bind the FTC or the public. In any enforcement action, the Commission must prove the challenged act or practice violates one or more existing statutory or regulatory requirements. In addition, this Policy Statement does not preempt federal, state, or local laws. Compliance with those laws, however, will not necessarily preclude Commission law enforcement action under the FTC Act or other statutes." The same footnote records that the Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs designated the Policy Statement as not a "major rule" as defined by 5 U.S.C. 804(2). The quoted passages are taken from the Introduction and the opening of Part II of the document; the remainder of the document was not read.

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission's proposed rule "Conflicts and Affiliations" was published in the Federal Register on August 6, 2026 as document 2026-15948, RIN 3038-AF76. The DATES section states: "Comments must be in writing and received by October 5, 2026." Comments are to be identified by "Conflicts and Affiliations" and RIN 3038-AF76.
