2026-07-11 · OCC / FDIC / NCUA / U.S. Treasury / FinCEN / OFAC

GENIUS Act stablecoin rulemaking deadline (July 18, 2026) one week out — six agencies had not finalized implementing rules as of July 11

As of July 11, 2026, six federal agencies — the OCC, FDIC, NCUA, U.S. Treasury, FinCEN, and OFAC — faced a statutory July 18, 2026 deadline (one year after the GENIUS Act's July 18, 2025 enactment) to finalize implementing rules for permitted payment stablecoin issuers (PPSIs), and had not yet issued final rules. Comment periods on the proposed rules closed by early June 2026. The proposed framework requires PPSIs to hold 1:1 reserves in cash, insured deposits, and short-term U.S. Treasuries, and treats issuers as financial institutions under the Bank Secrecy Act. 2026-07-14 · CFTC (Commodity Futures Trading Commission)

CFTC invokes emergency authority to stay KalshiEX's own rule change and orders fulfillment of Michigan-resident trades — first federal override targeting a DCM's state-court-directed rule amendment

On July 14, 2026, the CFTC issued Press Release 9267-26, announcing it had exercised emergency authority under the Commodity Exchange Act to stay a KalshiEX rule change and order KalshiEX to fulfill pending trades. Background: a Michigan state court issued a temporary restraining order directing KalshiEX to cancel all executed trades by Michigan-resident customers; KalshiEX filed an emergency rule change with the CFTC to comply with that state court order. The CFTC stayed the KalshiEX rule change and ordered KalshiEX to fulfill all pending trades per normal exchange practices. CFTC Chairman Brian Quintenz Selig stated the action was 'unprecedented,' noting Michigan is 'the first state to attempt to directly interfere with already-executed transactions on a federally regulated exchange.' As of the release, the CFTC has filed preemption suits against nine states: Arizona, Connecticut, Illinois, Kentucky, Minnesota, New Mexico, New York, Rhode Island, and Wisconsin.

2026-07-10 · Arizona Department of Gaming

Arizona Department of Gaming serves cease-and-desist orders on five online gambling operators, alleging felony money laundering and gambling promotion

On July 10, 2026, the Arizona Department of Gaming (ADG) issued cease-and-desist orders to BetOpenly, Bookmaker, Club WPT Online Poker, Kutt Inc., and Raffle Creator. Each order alleges three Arizona felonies under A.R.S. Title 13, Chapter 33: (1) promotion of gambling, (2) illegal control of an enterprise, and (3) money laundering. All five are directed to immediately cease all gambling operations in Arizona. Future actions may include criminal charges, civil suits, restitution, and forfeiture. ADG Director Jackie Johnson stated: 'Arizona is taking decisive action against illegal gambling operators that put Arizonans at risk.' This action expands ADG's 2025 sweepstakes-casino enforcement pattern to peer-to-peer wagering platforms, offshore sportsbooks, and unlicensed poker operators.
